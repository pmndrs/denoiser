denoiser · upscale pipeline

Three pmndrs packages on one GPUDevice: three.js renders a noisy scene at low resolution, denoiser cleans it, and @pmndrs/upscaler (AMD FSR-style) upscales it to full canvas resolution — no CPU pixel copies in the chain. Each GPU texture is handed straight to the next stage on the device the denoiser created.

Temporal (FSR3), the default, renders every frame under a different sub-pixel camera jitter and accumulates those frames into a display-resolution history, reprojected with depth + motion vectors from the same render. Because each frame samples different points inside a pixel, it reconstructs detail the low-res render never had in any single frame — and it also averages away the frame-to-frame residue the denoiser leaves. Spatial (FSR1) is one frame in, one frame out: edge-adaptive upsampling plus sharpening, no history, so it can only sharpen what is already there.

What to look for: switch between the two on a still view (drag once to stop the spin). Temporal: thin curves of the knot and the sphere silhouettes stay smooth and steady, specular highlights stop crawling, and the flat floor and background stop "boiling". Spatial: sharp, but the denoiser's frame-to-frame residue shows as a grainy shimmer on every surface, and edges keep their render-resolution stepping. While the knot spins, temporal relies on motion vectors — watch the knot's edges for any trailing. Try Denoise off too: temporal accumulation alone already averages the per-frame noise.
