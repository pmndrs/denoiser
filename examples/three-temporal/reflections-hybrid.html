The room from the shadows demo, with reflections built the ways engines build them: a reflection probe (a cubemap captured once, box-projected, no rays), three's screen-space reflections, rays traced against the scene with three-mesh-bvh, and the hybrid: probe on rough surfaces, SSR on glossy ones, a traced ray where SSR misses. Every stochastic mode takes one sample per pixel, cleaned up by ffxReflections(). Watch the turning sculpture in the table and the floor: the probe froze it when it was baked.

What each mode gets right

  • Probe: free per frame and noise-free, fine on rough surfaces. But it's a snapshot from one point: moving things freeze, and box projection only lines up for the box's walls, so the table legs reflect in the wrong place.
  • SSR: exact positions and live objects, but only for what's on screen. Anything off screen or hidden falls back to the probe.
  • Ray traced: exact and live, on screen or not. It costs a ray per pixel plus shading at the hit, and needs a denoiser.
  • Hybrid: spends rays only where SSR can't answer and the surface is glossy enough to show the difference.
  • Why the cutoff: switch to SSR or ray traced and look at the rug. One sample per pixel on a fully rough surface is mostly noise, and FFX's reflection denoiser is built for glossy surfaces. AMD's own SSSR sends rough pixels to the probe for the same reason.

How the traced hits are shaded

  • The hit's albedo and metalness ride along in the BVH as a per-vertex attribute.
  • Direct light from the panel with one shadow ray, ambient from the same environment the raster pass uses. Metals mirror the environment. No second bounce into the scene.
  • That's the usual trade in hybrid renderers: cheap, approximate shading at the hit. The reference uses the same shading with many rays, so it shows where the 1-sample modes miss, not a path-traced ground truth.
