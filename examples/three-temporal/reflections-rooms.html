Reflections in two rooms: probes, SSR and rays

Reflection probes give every room its own reflections for almost nothing per frame: one probe per room, one in the doorway. They can't show what moves, where things touch, or what the probe never saw. SSR and one traced ray per pixel, denoised by ffxReflections(), cover that. The scene is the two rooms from Ben Houston's reflection probes example for three.js, with a polished floor. How it fits together

Probes: the base layer

A probe is six renders of the room from one point (a cubemap), pre-blurred for each roughness. Each probe has a box: surfaces inside it use it, and its reflections are projected onto the room's walls. Smaller boxes win where boxes overlap, so the doorway probe takes over between the rooms, and the edges blend.

  • Every pixel gets its own room's reflection, at any roughness, with no noise and no rays.
  • The probes also light the materials: their diffuse ambient comes from the room's probe.
  • They are snapshots. When the lights change, they are stale until re-baked. Here a re-bake of all three is about 2 ms, so with "Live capture" on the page re-bakes them every frame the room changes.

SSR and rays: what probes can't do

Below the roughness cutoff, each pixel takes one random direction in its reflection lobe per frame. SSR looks for it in the frame already rendered. Where SSR finds nothing, a ray is traced against the scene's triangles (a BVH from three-mesh-bvh) and shaded at the hit, with the probes as the hit's ambient light. ffxReflections() turns the one noisy sample per pixel into a clean image.

  • Moving objects: the mirror ball is kept out of the probes, and still shows up in the floor.
  • Contact and furniture: reflections start where things touch the floor.
  • Rough surfaces stay on the probes: one sample there is mostly noise, and the blur hides the probe's errors.

How this demo is built

  • Probes: our port of three.js #35046 to three r186 (see reflection probes on r186). The materials get them as scene.environmentNode; the reflection passes sample them at any point with sampleReflectionProbes().
  • The room animates as in the PR's example: the lights drift through the hues and the knot turns. Both are in the captures; the mirror ball is hidden during a bake, like the moving objects in engines.
  • SSR: three's SSRNode. Rays: three-mesh-bvh's WebGPU BVH, refitted every frame for the ball and the turning knot. Hits get direct light from both lamps (one shadow ray each), no highlights.
  • The same pipeline as the one-room reflections explainer, which compares each method on its own.

When to add what

  • Rooms, corridors, interiors: a probe per space, plus one at each opening. Cheap, and right for everything rough and everything on the walls.
  • Glossy floors, things that move: add SSR. It is cheap and exact for what is on screen.
  • Mirrors, contact, anything off screen: add rays where SSR misses, and a denoiser.
  • Lights that change: re-bake on change if you can afford it, or a probe per frame; SSR and rays stay live either way.
