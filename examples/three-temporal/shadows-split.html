Ray-traced shadows and reflections in a raster app

A normal real-time three.js scene (WebGPU raster, 60 fps) where two effects are ray traced at one ray per pixel: the shadow of a big soft light, and glossy reflections. One ray is far too noisy to look at. ffxShadows() and ffxReflections() from denoiser/three (AMD FidelityFX denoisers, ported to WGSL) clean it up every frame. When to use this

  • You ship a real-time raster app (a game, a product configurator, an archviz walkthrough) that has to hold 60 fps while the camera moves.
  • You want one effect raster can't fake well: soft area-light shadows that stay sharp where objects touch the ground and soften with distance, or glossy reflections of the actual scene instead of an env map.
  • So you trace 1 ray per pixel for just that effect, and let the denoiser turn the noise into a clean, stable result every frame.

When to use OIDN instead

  • You path trace the whole frame (all lighting, bounces, glass), for example a product render or a viewer that refines when the camera stops.
  • That's Denoiser, the package's main API (OIDN): one whole image, no history needed, tens of milliseconds per denoise. Run it on a still frame or every few frames, not every frame at 60 fps.
  • FFX does the opposite: one effect, every frame, about 0.5 ms (shadows) or 1.7 ms (reflections) at 1080p, using motion vectors and history.

What it costs

  • Ray tracing: 1 ray per pixel against the scene's BVH. Here that's three-mesh-bvh's WebGPU traversal in a full-screen TSL pass, no ray-tracing hardware needed. The table above shows what it costs for this scene.
  • Denoising: FFX shadows about 0.5 ms and FFX reflections about 1.7 ms at 1080p (Apple M-series). The cost depends on resolution, not on the scene.
  • No precomputation or baking. Lights, objects and the camera can all move.

What the denoiser needs from you

  • From your G-buffer: depth, normals (view or world, packed is fine), motion vectors (three's velocity in the MRT), and roughness for reflections.
  • An honest 1-ray signal: new random samples every frame, not pre-blurred. For shadows that's 0 or 1 per pixel. For reflections it's the radiance plus the hit distance (three's stochastic SSR gives you both).
  • Call resetHistory() on camera cuts and teleports, or the old frame bleeds into the new one.
// visibility: a TSL node or texture, 1 = lit, 0 = shadowed, 1 ray/pixel
const shadows = ffxShadows(visibility, {
  camera, depth: scenePass.getTextureNode('depth'), velocity: scenePass.getTextureNode('velocity'),
  normal: scenePass.getTextureNode('normal'), normalSpace: 'view', normalEncoding: 'packed',
});
const lit = sceneColor.add(albedo.mul(irradiance).mul(shadows.r));
